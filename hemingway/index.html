Hemingway Didn’t Say That

Oft-cited aphorisms traced to their sources.

Opening

Behold: four popular quotes, four famous names. Before we trust any of them, we ought to confirm whom they came from.

Method

  1. Search the exact wording; use quotation marks to keep your results verbatim.
  2. Find the quote’s earliest dated appearance; note attribution including who, where, and when.
  3. Compare wording. Change over time is important evidence.
  4. Check Quote Investigator last to test your findings (not to replace them! Our work needn’t appear flawless!).

Case 1

“Write drunk, edit sober.”

Typically credited to Ernest Hemingway.

Trail log | Case 1
Reveal the trail

No evidence connects the line to Hemingway. Novelist Peter De Vries gave a character the line "Sometimes I write drunk and revise sober" in Reuben, Reuben (1964).

Case 2

“Be the change you wish to see in the world.”

Typically credited to Mahatma Gandhi.

Trail log | Case 2
Reveal the trail

The slogan doesn't pop up in any of Gandhi's known writings. The closest verified line (circa 1913) reads "If we could change ourselves, the tendencies in the world would also change." Quote Investigator traces the short version to educator Arleen Lorrance in 1974.

Case 3

“Well-behaved women seldom make history.”

Typically credited to Marilyn Monroe or Eleanor Roosevelt (fun pair!).

Trail log | Case 3
Reveal the trail

Historian Laurel Thatcher Ulrich wrote it in a 1976 article in American Quarterly. The line traveled for years without her name.

Case 4

“Reports of my death are greatly exaggerated.”

Typically credited to Mark Twain.

Trail log | Case 4
Reveal the trail

Twain said it, but not in those words. The New York Journal of June 2, 1897, printed "The report of my death was an exaggeration." Subsequent retellings buffed it to a shine.

Practice

Rewrite one quotation the right way: exact words, original speaker, date, and specific source.

Model: In a 1976 article in American Quarterly, historian Laurel Thatcher Ulrich wrote, “Well-behaved women seldom make history.”
