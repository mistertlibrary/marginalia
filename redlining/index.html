Mapping Inequality

A tour of Essex County as the federal government graded it in c. 1939.

Opening

Circa 1939, federal appraisers graded every neighborhood in Essex County, all four of our sending communities included. Let’s read what they wrote, then investigate the consequences.

Method

  1. Try to locate West Essex High and each of the sending districts on the map. Note each area’s grade and color.
  2. Read the agents’ notes for the four areas below. Underline everything they measured.
  3. Compare! Which details decided each grade: the houses, the prices, or the people?
  4. Read the two economists’ findings. Decide which the evidence better supports. What sort of evidence would help clarify the matter?
Open the Essex County map

Evidence 1

A-30, Essex Fells (A, “1st”)

“It is magnificent country and far enough from crowded centers to attract wealthy seekers after substantial suburban homes.”

Evidence 2

C-42, Caldwell Borough (C, “3rd”)

“Caldwell is a very old settlement of which this area is one of the older portions.” Agents recorded 0% Black residents and no “infiltration.”

Evidence 3

A-23, Montclair South Side (A, “1st”)

“Cross St. which is a short block running from Harrison to Orange Rd. contains a few houses that have been occupied by negroes for many years, but there is no apparent possibility of spreading.”

Evidence 4

D-21, Montclair South Side (D, “4th”)

“This district houses the greater part of Montclair’s large negro population. Most of these are employed locally as domestics, gardeners, chauffeurs, etc.” Agents recorded 90% Black residents, 30% home ownership, and mortgage funds: “None.”

The Economists

Side A

Daniel Aaronson, Daniel Hartley, and Bhashkar Mazumder (2021) compared neighborhoods on either side of HOLC boundary lines. They found the maps led to lower home ownership, house values, and rents, and to more racial segregation in later decades.

Side B

Price Fishback, Jessica LaVoice, Allison Shertzer, and Randall Walsh (2023) found that racial bias in drawing the maps explains at most 4 to 20 percent of the concentration of Black households in the lowest-rated zones. They also found the Federal Housing Administration relied relatively little on HOLC maps.

Discussion

Exit Ticket

For teachers

The 1930s area descriptions use openly racist, antisemitic, and nativist language; agents were asked to measure the “infiltration” of Black and foreign-born residents. We should spend some time with the Essex County descriptions before class and frame them as evidence of federal policy.
